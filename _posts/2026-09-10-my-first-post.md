When I joined the Libon Baby Lab for my first postdoc, I started working with Neuroscan EEG systems and CURRY software for EEG data recording. I was in charge of running the EEG component of a gamified phonetic training study with Portuguese learners of English. In particular, I was interested in seeing the effects of gamified phonetic training on the Mismatch Negativity (MMN) responses of our learners.

During my PhD I had collected plenty of EEG data using Biosemi systems which use Actiview as their recording software so learning CURRY8, the version available at the Lisbon lab, took some time. CURRY8 comes with a data-analysis extension but due to the time constraints of the project, I decided to use MATLAB to analyse the data, as I had already used MATLAB during my PhD. This blog post describes how to use the scripts in my mmn-gamified-phonetic-training repository to analyse MMN data and aims to guide researchers who are transitioning to CURRY for EEG data collection or those who are new to working with MMN responses.
 
Firstly, EEGLAB has a currydata plugin which you will find on Github. You will need to download the plugin and save it in your EEGLAB plugins folder. Make sure you include all the appropriate folders on your MATLAB path and then, you're ready to run step 01 of the mmn-gamified-phonetic-training pipeline. Make sure you adapt the script to let MATLAB know where your EEG data files are saved. The first step of the pipeline applies a 0.1 Hz high-pass filter to each recording block separately, rather than filtering the entire recording in one go. This approach helps to avoid edge artefacts at block boundaries. Before any channels are removed, the script saves the VEOG signal so that it can later be used to identify eye-blink related components. The VEOG and trigger channels are then removed, standard 10-20 electrode locations are assigned, and channel labels are converted from the NeuroScan naming convention (e.g., FZ, FCZ) to the mixed-case format used by GEDAI, the data-cleaning method I found most appropriate for my data, and FieldTrip (e.g., Fz, FCz). Finally, the processed data are saved as EEGLAB .set files together with MATLAB snapshots of the processed EEG and the separately stored VEOG signal.  

The second step of the pipeline involves artefact and rereferencing. It takes the filtered files and first applies GEDAI (Generalised Eigenvalue Decomposition for Artefact Identification) to reduce non-neural noise in the data. ICA is then run on the GEDAI-cleaned recordings, and ICLabel (from EEGLAB) is used to identify eye-blink related components. Importantly, components are only removed when they are classified as eye activity with high confidence and show the expected frontopolar scalp distribution. Although the VEOG signal saved in the previous step is loaded by the script, it is not used to drive artefact rejection. Instead, it is used as a post-ICA quality check by calculating residual correlations with the cleaned data. Finally, the EEG is rereferenced using the robust rereferencing method implemented in NoiseTools and saved for the subsequent stages of the MMN analysis pipeline. 

The third script extracts the ERP responses used to calculate the MMN. It starts by loading the artefact-cleaned EEG data produced in the previous step and applies a 30 Hz low-pass filter, which is commonly used for visualising MMN waveforms. The data are then segmented into epochs around standard and deviant stimuli, using a 200 ms pre-stimulus period for baseline correction. Subject-level ERPs are created by averaging all standard and deviant trials separately, after which the MMN difference wave is calculated by subtracting the standard response from the deviant response (DEV − STD). These individual difference waves are saved for later statistical analyses, and grand-average ERPs and MMN responses are also computed across participants. To ensure that all participants can be included in the group averages, the script uses only channels that are available for every dataset, which accommodates cases where a channel may have been removed during preprocessing. Finally, the script generates plots of the group-average standard and deviant ERPs together with the MMN waveform, allowing a quick visual inspection of the expected negative deflection typically observed between approximately 100 and 250 ms at frontocentral electrodes.
